# Planning Mandate
## Republic of Kanyisa

---

# Purpose

The Republic of Kanyisa undertakes integrated electricity planning to ensure that sufficient electricity infrastructure is developed to support economic growth, social development, environmental sustainability, and long-term energy security.

Electricity planning is intended to identify least-regret pathways for developing the power system while balancing affordability, reliability, sustainability, and national development objectives.

The planning process provides guidance for investment decisions, infrastructure development, policy formulation, and procurement activities across the electricity sector.

---

# Legislative Basis

The National Electricity Plan (NEP) is prepared under the authority of the Ministry of Energy and Minerals (MEM) and implemented through the institutions of the Kanyisan electricity sector.

The planning process is supported by:

- National Development Vision 2050 (NDV 2050)
- Integrated Energy Strategy (IES)
- Just Transition Framework (JTF)
- Electricity sector regulations
- Environmental legislation
- National climate commitments

---

# Planning Objective

The primary objective of electricity planning in Kanyisa is:

> To ensure that present and future electricity demand is supplied reliably, affordably, sustainably, and at least cost to society.

Achievement of this objective requires balancing multiple competing priorities rather than optimizing a single metric.

---

# Core Planning Principles

## Security of Supply

The electricity system shall maintain sufficient generation, transmission, storage, and operational reserves to meet expected demand under a range of future conditions.

Planning must avoid persistent energy shortages and unacceptable reliability risks.

---

## Affordability

Electricity should remain affordable for households, businesses, and industrial consumers.

Investment decisions shall consider:

- Capital costs
- Operating costs
- Fuel costs
- System integration costs
- Long-term consumer impacts

---

## Economic Development

The electricity sector shall support national economic growth by:

- Enabling industrial development
- Supporting mining and manufacturing
- Expanding electricity access
- Facilitating job creation
- Improving competitiveness

---

## Sustainability

Planning shall consider environmental impacts and support the transition toward lower-emission technologies where economically and technically feasible.

---

## Technology Neutrality

Technology options shall be evaluated based on transparent assumptions and objective criteria.

No technology shall be included or excluded without an explicit justification.

---

## Transparency

All assumptions, methodologies, data sources, and scenario definitions should be documented and publicly accessible.

---

# Planning Questions

The planning process seeks to answer the following questions:

## Capacity Requirements

- How much new capacity is required?
- When is new capacity needed?
- What reserve margin should be maintained?

---

## Technology Deployment

- Which technologies should be developed?
- What combination of technologies minimizes system costs?
- How does technology choice change under different futures?

---

## Infrastructure Development

- What transmission investments are required?
- Where should infrastructure be located?
- Which regions require network reinforcement?

---

## Reliability

- Can future demand be supplied reliably?
- Are adequate operating reserves available?
- Can the system withstand supply disruptions?

---

## Decarbonisation

- What pathways exist to reduce emissions?
- What are the costs of decarbonisation?
- What trade-offs emerge under emissions constraints?

---

# Scope of Planning

Electricity planning considers the following components.

## Demand

Forecasts of future electricity consumption.

Including:

- Residential demand
- Commercial demand
- Industrial demand
- Mining demand
- Agricultural demand
- Transport electrification

---

## Supply

Evaluation of:

- Existing generation assets
- New generation technologies
- Retirement schedules
- Fuel supply options
- Regional imports

---

## Transmission

Assessment of:

- Grid expansion requirements
- Congestion risks
- Connection needs
- Regional interconnections

---

## Storage and Flexibility

Including:

- Battery storage
- Pumped storage
- Flexible generation
- Demand-side flexibility

---

# Decision Criteria

Planning outcomes are evaluated against multiple criteria.

## Cost

Metrics may include:

- Net Present Cost
- System Cost
- Marginal Cost of Supply
- Capital Investment Requirements

---

## Reliability

Metrics may include:

- Planning Reserve Margin
- Expected Unserved Energy
- Capacity Adequacy
- Peak Demand Coverage

---

## Environmental Performance

Metrics may include:

- Carbon emissions
- Emissions intensity
- Water usage
- Land requirements

---

## Economic Outcomes

Metrics may include:

- Electricity prices
- Investment requirements
- Employment impacts
- Industrial competitiveness

---

# Planning Horizon

The default planning horizon for the Kanyisa Toolkit is:

**2025–2050**

Annual investment and operational decisions are evaluated across the full planning period.

Long-term planning is intended to identify robust pathways rather than precise forecasts.

---

# Treatment of Uncertainty

Future developments cannot be predicted with certainty.

Therefore, planning shall consider alternative assumptions relating to:

- Electricity demand growth
- Fuel prices
- Technology costs
- Carbon policies
- Economic growth
- Renewable resource availability
- Regional trade opportunities

Scenarios are used to evaluate the resilience of planning outcomes under different futures.

---

# Role of Modelling

Energy system models are used to support planning by:

- Testing alternative futures
- Evaluating investment pathways
- Quantifying trade-offs
- Identifying least-cost solutions
- Assessing policy impacts

Models are decision-support tools rather than decision-makers.

Final planning decisions remain the responsibility of policymakers and sector institutions.
